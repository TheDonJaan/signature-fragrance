# Toronto fragrance decant storefront — market research & launch economics

**Research date:** 29 October 2026  
**Prototype working brand:** SIGNATURE FRAGRANCE  
**Market:** Toronto / GTA, with a deliberate medium-to-high gross-margin strategy.

## Executive conclusion

The strongest position is **not** “cheapest decants in Canada.” The Canadian market already contains hobby/community decanters and low-overhead sellers that are difficult to beat on raw $/mL. The more defensible Toronto offer is: **authenticity + presentation + curation + fast GTA access + a frictionless guest checkout + recurring discovery**.

The launch catalog should use the existing 28-bottle wardrobe as a deliberately curated first drop rather than pretend to be a 500-fragrance warehouse. Value bottles should generate very high contribution margins; premium bottles such as Nishane, Tom Ford, Xerjoff and Chanel should work as trust/traffic anchors with lower percentage margins and explicit subscription surcharges.

## Market benchmarks observed

- **ScentClub Canada:** 8 mL monthly decant from **$19.95**; two 8 mL scents **$34.95**; three **$49.95**, with shipping included. This defines an important subscription-value anchor.
- **ScentBloom:** 1, 2, 5 and 10 mL; entry samples advertised from **$1.50 CAD**, creating a low-price discovery reference point.
- **Atelier Elemental:** examples included **Dior Sauvage EDP 2 mL at $10**, **Xerjoff Naxos 2 mL at $14.99**, and **Creed Aventus 2 mL at $18.99**.
- **Scent Legacy:** a simplified premium grid around **$12 / 2 mL, $25 / 5 mL, $45 / 10 mL** on several headline scents.
- **Arôme Essence:** many niche 2 mL samples cluster around roughly **$18–$30**.
- Current Canadian product results for exact/near-exact launch scents included: **Wulong Cha $16 / 2 mL and ~$56–$65 / 10 mL**, **Hacivat $16 / 2 mL and ~$60 / 10 mL**, **Torino23 roughly $22 / 2 mL, $47 / 5 mL and $84 / 10 mL**, **Tom Ford Oud Minérale around $15 / 2 mL, $32 / 5 mL and $54 / 10 mL**, and **SNOI around $30 / 10 mL**.

### What the spread means

Pricing is not governed by one clean $/mL rule. Brand prestige, replacement cost, decant size, packaging quality, trust, availability and seller reputation all move the market. That supports a **tiered catalog**, not a universal multiplier.

## Recommended price architecture

The prototype uses four tiers:

| Tier | Job | Typical 2 mL | Typical 5 mL | Typical 10 mL | Subscription treatment |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Core | Margin engine / easy add-on | $5–$6 | $10–$12 | $18–$21 | Included |
| Select | Mainstream designer / stronger cost | $8–$11 | $16–$22 | $28–$39 | +$2 per 5 mL slot |
| Premium | Niche or costly juice | $11–$16 | $22–$34 | $39–$59 | +$6 per 5 mL slot |
| Reserve | Very costly / prestige traffic | $15–$22 | $31–$47 | up to $84; some sizes omitted | +$11 per 5 mL slot; excluded from 10 mL subscription |

This intentionally earns the highest percentage margins on inexpensive bottles while keeping prestige decants inside recognizable Canadian market ranges.

## Subscription model

Recommended launch plans used in the prototype:

1. **Explorer — $36/month:** 2 × 5 mL.
2. **Rotation — $49/month:** 3 × 5 mL. This is the strongest acquisition plan because it lands near the Canadian subscription benchmark while creating three discovery moments.
3. **Travel Duo — $58/month:** 2 × 10 mL. Select and Premium bottles carry larger surcharges; Reserve-tier 10 mL choices are excluded from this plan to prevent destructive subscription economics.

The subscription should not promise that every scent is included at one flat price. Premium surcharges are strategically important: without them, a customer can repeatedly select the highest-cost Xerjoff/Nishane/Chanel inventory and destroy blended margin.

A good production rule is **no forced account**. Use guest checkout and send a signed/secure “manage subscription” link by email. This preserves convenience without creating password friction.

## AOV strategy

Single-decant orders are structurally weak because pick/pack, payment fees and compliant parcel movement do not fall in proportion to bottle size. The prototype therefore emphasizes:

- **Toronto Flight — $49 for 5 × 2 mL** Core/Select scents, with premium upgrades later.
- Free pickup as the default low-cost fulfillment option.
- Bundles and subscriptions before discount codes.
- Free local delivery only above a future threshold (recommended test range: **$65–$85**).
- Do not offer “free shipping everywhere” until actual dangerous-goods carrier cost is known.

## Packaging economics

Current sourcing research showed very large variance in atomizer cost. Examples ranged from bulk 5 mL glass sprayers around **$0.40–$0.70 each** on marketplace bulk packs to Canadian packaging suppliers around **$1.50+ for 10 mL** glass atomizers. The margin model uses conservative direct handling allowances rather than the absolute cheapest listing:

- 2 mL atomizer: $0.35 planning allowance
- 5 mL atomizer: $0.50
- 10 mL atomizer: $0.80
- label: $0.10
- filling/handling allocation: $0.65 / $0.75 / $0.90 by size
- juice shrinkage/transfer loss allowance: **7%**

The CSV included with this build shows the bottle-by-bottle planning margins. These are **gross margins before order-level box/cushioning, payment processing, delivery subsidy, refunds/damage and tax**. Replace every planning cost with your real invoice cost before launch.

## Fulfillment and dangerous-goods constraint

Alcohol-based perfume is a **Class 3 flammable liquid**. Canada Post publicly states that ordinary alcohol-based perfume is dangerous goods/non-mailable in the normal stream, while its dangerous-goods guidance also describes an expanded limited/excepted-quantity program for eligible contract or Solutions for Small Business customers. Purolator explicitly lists perfume as dangerous goods and provides compliant dangerous-goods services, including ground options, subject to account/packaging/acceptance rules.

**Launch implication:** treat ordinary lettermail as an unacceptable business assumption. Start with GTA pickup/local delivery and establish a documented limited-quantity/ground workflow with a carrier before enabling national shipping.

## Canadian cosmetics / labelling constraint

Perfume is a cosmetic under the Food and Drugs Act. Health Canada states that cosmetics sold in Canada are subject to the Cosmetic Regulations and that manufacturers/importers have notification obligations. Cosmetic labels generally require the product identity, metric net quantity, dealer identity/place of business, contact information, ingredient list using INCI nomenclature, and required warnings/directions; most mandatory information is bilingual. New fragrance-allergen disclosure requirements also took effect in 2026.

**Important:** repackaging a branded fragrance into your own decant format raises a real compliance question around who is the responsible manufacturer/notifier for the decanted product. Do not assume the original brand’s notification automatically solves the obligations for your new package/label. Confirm the exact decanter obligations with Health Canada or Canadian regulatory counsel before taking real orders.

## Ontario tax / subscription considerations

- Ontario taxable supplies generally use **13% HST**.
- CRA’s small-supplier threshold is generally **$30,000** of taxable supplies under the applicable four-quarter/single-quarter tests; voluntary registration may be possible below it.
- Online/recurring sales need clear total price, payment terms, delivery/performance terms, cancellation mechanics and a retrievable copy of the agreement under Ontario consumer-protection rules.

For the prototype, prices are shown pre-tax and recurring billing is deliberately disabled.

## Recommended launch sequencing

1. Launch **local pickup + controlled GTA delivery**, not Canada-wide mail.
2. Sell the existing wardrobe as a curated “Drop 01” of 28 scents.
3. Put **Rotation (3 × 5 mL / $49)** at the center of the homepage.
4. Use the cheapest-to-replace Core scents as bundle-margin engines.
5. Keep Xerjoff, Nishane, Chanel, Tom Ford and Sauvage Elixir as Premium/Reserve with explicit upgrades.
6. Add payment only after labels, carrier handling and terms are production-ready.
7. After 30–60 paid orders, use sell-through by scent and size—not social hype—to decide what bottles to restock/add.
